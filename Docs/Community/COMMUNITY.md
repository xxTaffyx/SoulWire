## What This Division Is

The technology industry has a geography problem. Its headquarters cluster in the same cities, its products are designed for the same demographics, and the places it ignores — rural communities, Appalachian towns, underserved populations far from the coasts — are not accidents of the market. They are consequences of choices made by people who never had to live in those places.

SoulWire Community exists to make different choices. Not charity. Not parachute philanthropy where an outside organization decides what a community needs and leaves when the cameras go. Genuine investment. Long-term presence. Building the infrastructure and then transferring ownership so the community doesn't need SoulWire to maintain it.

The question driving every Community Division decision: **what does this place need, and can we build it with the people who live here instead of for them?**

---

## Appalachia First

The Appalachian region is where the Community Division anchors its work. Not because Appalachian communities are more deserving than other underserved communities — they are not a competition for need — but because SoulWire's founder has roots there, understands what was taken from those places, and believes that understanding matters when you are trying to help without harming.

Coal companies did not leave those mountains out of market logic. They left because they had extracted what they came for. The communities that built their economies around extraction were left with polluted water, damaged land, unemployment, and a federal and corporate indifference that treated the people there as a resource that had been used up.

SoulWire comes to Appalachia with the opposite model. We build things we intend to leave behind — owned by the communities, maintained by the communities, generating value for the communities indefinitely after SoulWire moves to the next project.

## Hillbilly Days — Pikeville, Kentucky

Hillbilly Days is the largest festival in eastern Kentucky. Every April, Pikeville draws 100,000 people who come because this place and these people are worth celebrating.

SoulWire anchors its Appalachian community presence at Hillbilly Days — not as a vendor or a sponsor seeking brand exposure, but as a participant. A presence. A member of the community showing up because the community is worth showing up for.

**What SoulWire does at Hillbilly Days:**

| Activity | What It Is |
|----------|-----------|
| **SoulWire Demo Station** | Hands-on with SoulWire apps — no sales pressure, just access |
| **Device Lending Program** | Borrow a SoulPhone for the weekend, experience the full ecosystem |
| **Tech Help Tent** | Free tech support for anything — not just SoulWire products |
| **Listening Sessions** | What do people here actually need? What problems exist that SoulWire hasn't thought of? |
| **Hiring Fair** | SoulWire jobs that can be done remotely from Appalachia — presented to people who live here |
| **Community Board** | Public display of what SoulWire has committed to in the region and the status of those commitments |

The Community Board is the accountability mechanism. Every commitment SoulWire makes to Appalachian communities is posted publicly at Hillbilly Days and updated every year. If something was promised and not delivered, it is on the board. No exceptions.

## What SoulWire Builds in Underserved Communities

### Connectivity Infrastructure

A SoulWire app running on spotty or nonexistent internet is a broken promise. Before the apps, the connection. The Community Division works with local governments and community organizations to establish reliable broadband infrastructure in areas the commercial internet market has decided aren't profitable enough to serve.

SoulWire does not own the infrastructure it installs. It is built in partnership with local entities — co-ops, municipal governments, community organizations — who own it going forward. SoulWire provides the technical expertise and initial capital. The community provides the governance.

### Device Access Programs

Having the software means nothing without the hardware. The Community Division runs device access programs — refurbished SoulPhones, subsidized new devices for households below income thresholds, device lending libraries at community centers and libraries.

The goal is that no one in a community SoulWire serves is unable to access SoulWire's ecosystem because of the cost of a device.

### Energy Infrastructure

Partnered directly with the [[ENERGY|Energy Division]]. Community microgrids, SoulSolar installations, and SoulSolar Anchor single-home systems installed in communities where energy access is unreliable or unaffordable. The Appalachian energy priority work is coordinated between this division and Energy.

### Food Infrastructure

Partnered with [[NOURISH|Nourish]]. Vertical farming installations in communities with food access deserts. Community kitchen infrastructure. Cooking education. The goal is that no community SoulWire serves has worse food access after SoulWire's presence than before it.

### Creek and River Restoration

Appalachian waterways have been damaged by mining runoff, industrial waste, and the general indifference of extractive industries toward the ecosystems they operate in. The Community Division partners with [[EARTHGUARD|EarthGuard]] and local environmental organizations on creek restoration projects.

This is not symbolic. Clean water is health infrastructure. Communities that have watched their creeks run orange from acid mine drainage for decades are watching SoulWire show up with different intentions.

*"While they destroy, SoulWire cleans and helps."*

## The Hiring Commitment

SoulWire actively recruits from the communities it serves. Remote-capable positions — which is most SoulWire positions — are presented to community members first. SoulWire provides training and apprenticeship for people who want to work in technology but have had no path into it.

This is not a quota system. It is a preference. When two equally qualified candidates exist, the one from a community SoulWire has committed to building gets the job. When a community member is close to qualified, SoulWire provides the training to close the gap.

The tech industry's hiring geography problem — everyone from the same cities, the same schools, the same demographic backgrounds — produces products that reflect that narrowness. SoulWire believes the people who know what communities like Pikeville actually need should be involved in building the tools those communities will use.

## What This Division Is Not

The Community Division is not a marketing operation. Community presence is not brand building. If the presence doesn't create genuine value for the communities SoulWire is in, the presence stops.

The Community Division does not decide what communities need. It asks. Then it builds what's asked for. Communities that have been told by outside organizations what they need for generations deserve the respect of being listened to instead.

SoulWire does not leave when the project is done. There is no "done." There is ongoing presence, ongoing maintenance support, ongoing relationship. Extractive industries leave when the resource is exhausted. SoulWire is not here for a resource. It is here because these places deserve the same technology infrastructure that everywhere else takes for granted, and someone has to be the one to build it.
