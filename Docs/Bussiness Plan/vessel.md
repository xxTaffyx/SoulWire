# Family Vessels
## (Working Title -- Pending Final Name)

A SoulWire family vessel is a physical ship -- real, built, seaworthy or spaceworthy -- that belongs to a single family and is passed down through generations. It is not a product. It is not a rental. It is a legacy asset, like a family home that moves.

Every vessel carries the family's history inside it. Every vessel wears the family's identity on its hull. And every vessel, no matter where it travels -- across oceans, through orbit, or beyond -- remains connected to FamilyRoot, alive with the story of the people who came before and the people who sail it now.

---

## The Exterior

On the outside, every family vessel looks like what it is -- a ship. It does not broadcast wealth or status. It does not display personal information. It does not invite strangers into the family's story before they have been welcomed.

What it does carry is the family crest.

The crest is generated by FamilyRoot from the family's actual history -- DNA origins, generational patterns, meaningful symbols drawn from the real texture of who they are. It is not chosen from a menu. It is built from something true. For most families it will feel right immediately. For those who want to change it, a redesign request is submitted and the physical repaint is handled by a partnered maintenance crew at actual cost -- dry dock fees, hull prep, paint, labor. The process is real and the price reflects that. Changing a family crest is not a casual act. It should feel like the decision it is.

---

## The Interior

Inside the vessel is where the family lives. The exterior is neutral. The interior is entirely theirs.

The face wall is the first thing you see when you board. Every family member who has passed is represented here -- not as a photograph but as a living portrait. Their face. Their voice. Their own answers to the questions that matter. What was the hardest year of your life. What do you want the people who never met you to know. What did you hope the family would become.

These recordings are not automatic. They are intentional. SoulWire prompts living members once a year -- your family vessel is waiting for your recording. Every person who appears on that wall chose to be there. The intimacy of that choice is what makes it real.

The vessel connects to FamilyRoot continuously. New family members added to the tree appear in the system. When someone passes, their portrait transitions to its memorial state. The ship knows its own history because FamilyRoot feeds it, and the ship carries that history wherever it goes.

---

## Vessel Types

Families choose their vessel based on who they are and where they feel most at home. The founding generation makes this choice and it shapes the family's identity in the world for as long as the vessel exists.

The two primary types are ocean vessels and space vessels, reflecting the two frontiers SoulWire is built to follow humanity into. Within those categories families choose their purpose -- exploration, homesteading, voyaging, or simply having a home that moves.

The naming convention is House followed by the family name, followed by the vessel's given name. A vessel's name reflects its purpose and should sound like something worth inheriting. The Deepseeker. The Tidecaller. The Astronomer. The Wanderer. The Rootbearer. The Hearthkeeper. These are not product names. They are the names of ships that will outlast the people who first sailed them.

---

## Communal Vessels

Alongside personal family vessels, large communal ships exist for exploration, gathering, and discovery. These belong to no single family. They are the cities and ports of the ocean and space layers -- places where family vessels dock, where residents meet across family lines, where the world opens up beyond the horizon of any one story.

A family vessel is a home. A communal vessel is a destination. Both are necessary and neither replaces the other.

---

## The Digital Twin

Every physical vessel has a presence in Vortexian -- a digital twin that exists in the same form, in the same waters or space, accessible to family members who cannot be aboard the physical ship. Distance does not remove someone from the family vessel. They board the twin and they are home.

The twin updates in sync with the physical ship. New recordings appear on both face walls simultaneously. The family tree displayed in the interior reflects the same state in both versions. The ship is one thing existing in two places.

---

## Inheritance

A family vessel does not belong to one generation. It is built once and passed forward. Each generation adds to it -- their recordings on the face wall, their mark on the interior, their chapter in the ship's log. The vessel that a great-grandchild inherits is not the same ship that left the dock a hundred years before. It is more. It has been lived in by people they will come to know through the walls and the voices and the faces of everyone who sailed before them.

The vessel is the family in physical form. It goes where they go. It holds what they were. It carries what they will become.
